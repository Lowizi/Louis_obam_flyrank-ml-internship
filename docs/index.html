Understanding Content Decay

An analysis of search visibility signals using FlyRank's real search data warehouse, training random forest models to predict declining content and recommend prioritized review actions.

Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring Lane

Abstract

Question: Which safe content/search signals are associated with visibility decline in search results?

Method: Trained a Random Forest classifier on 30,000 pages from FlyRank's anonymized warehouse, using client-holdout validation (GroupKFold across all 32 clients) to avoid leakage.

Result: Model achieves P@50=0.58 against a 0.54 base rate — a modest but directional lift. Key drivers: engagement_rate, ctr, and log_impressions. 11/18 features overlap the 30-day label window (documented leakage risk).

Methodology

Problem Statement

Determining which pages are in decline and should be prioritized for content review or refresh — without client-identifying data or causal claims about Google's algorithm.

Data

  • Release: FlyRank ML Internship dataset — content_refresh_anonymized.csv
  • Size: 30,000+ rows after filtering (impressions_90d > 0, content_age_days >= 90)
  • Date window: Snapshot from — excluded pages younger than 90 days and zero-traffic pages
  • Exclusions: client-identifying fields removed; trend_pct, trend_direction, and other label siblings excluded as features

Features

engagement_rate

30-day engagement rate — top predictor of decline

ctr

Click-through rate from search impressions

log_impressions_90d

Cumulative search visibility over 90 days

content_age_days

Age of content in days — older pages show different patterns

avg_position

Average search position — declining positions correlate with decline label

ai_traffic_pct

Percentage of traffic from AI referrals — sparse but informative

Label Definition

is_declining = (trend_direction == "down") — computed from 30-day vs previous 30-day impression change. Same-window proxy: the label and many features use overlapping 30-day windows.

Baseline

Week-4 rule: stale * visible * log1p(impressions) where stale = content_age_days >= 180, visible = impressions_90d >= 500. Baseline P@50 ≈ 0.38 on same split.

Validation Design

  • GroupKFold by client_id — all 32 clients rotated through test once
  • Honest cross-client estimate (no single-draw optimism)
  • Compared against naive random-row split (inflates P@50 to 0.98 by leaking clients)

Leakage Checks

11 of 18 numeric features are 30/90-day aggregates that overlap the label's 30-day window. Reported as "legal to measure but window mismatch means model can learn trend direction from same window that produced it." Deliberately added raw trend_pct — score jumped to avg_pr=1.0, confirming the leak risk.

Key Findings

  • The random forest consistently outperforms the Week-4 baseline (0.58 vs 0.38 P@50) on the same grouped split
  • Logistic regression lags behind — the problem is non-linear (as expected)
  • Feature importance: engagement_rate > ctr > log_impressions drive predictions
  • Model errors: over-flagging old high-traffic pages (FP), under-flagging declining pages with good surface metrics (FN)

Base Rate Context

Every metric must be read against the 0.54 base rate. A P@50 of 0.58 represents a modest but meaningful directional lift — not a landslide. The naive random-row split would inflate this to 0.98 by allowing client leakage.

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Cross-sectional, not longitudinal: The model predicts "currently declining" not "will decline after refresh." Same-30-day window label limits future behavior prediction.
  • Single-draw optimism: W5's reported P@50=0.74 came from one client-holdout draw of 6 clients. The honest GroupKFold estimate across all 32 clients is P@50=0.58.
  • Survivor bias: Pages that remain in the snapshot at 365+ days are survivors — may not represent all old pages.
  • Label overlap leak: 11/18 features aggregate over windows that overlap the 30-day label formation window. Numbers are directional, not causal.
  • No causal claims: Observed associations ≠ refresh will recover traffic. The data shows correlation, not Google algorithm causality.

Writing the limitations section before a reader does is what earns trust. Negative results are results — "we expected X; the data shows no such pattern" is a valid and respect-earning sentence.

Ranked Recommendations

  1. Prioritize pages with low engagement_rate AND high content_age_days
    These are the highest-risk pages — old content that isn't engaging. Schedule a content review within 30 days.
  2. Flag pages with declining ctr but still-high impressions
    High-traffic pages losing clicks need immediate metadata review (title tags, meta descriptions).
  3. Monitor pages where log_impressions_90d is decreasing
    Visibility is dropping even if still high — early intervention before position degrades further.
  4. Review pages with ai_traffic_pct > 0 but low overall engagement
    AI referral traffic is sparse — these pages may need specialized content strategy, not generic refresh.
  5. Prune or merge very old pages (< 365 days) with zero 90-day traffic
    No survivor bias concern — these pages already have no traffic. Consider consolidation or removal.

Confidence levels: High for engagement_rate and ctr signals (observed patterns). Medium for age-based rules (directional, not causal). Low for AI-traffic-specific recommendations (sparse data, needs more study).
